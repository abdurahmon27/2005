**Depth: working, and no longer a side interest.** I came from the frontend; Go
is how I'm moving toward being a backend engineer who happens to be good at UI,
instead of a frontend engineer who dabbles. The move stopped being theoretical
when the backend became part of my job.

## Where it's real: dona.uz

At **[dona.uz](https://dona.uz)** I'm not picking the language for a toy. It's a
live-shopping marketplace — sellers broadcasting, buyers ordering mid-stream — so
the backend carries streams, orders and payments at the same time, and I'm
learning Go against that instead of against exercises.

Working across the whole thing helps more than I expected: I build the
[mobile app](/lab/skills/react-native), the seller panel and the services behind
them, so when an endpoint is awkward I'm usually the one who also feels it on the
client. That loop — design it, then have to consume it the next day — has taught
me more about API design than any style guide.

What production keeps teaching me that tutorials didn't:

- errors are a design surface, not an afterthought,
- `context` cancellation matters the moment a request can outlive its client,
- the boring solution survives the on-call rotation.

## Why Go

Because I wanted a language where the boring answer is usually the right one:
one binary, one way to format, a standard library that already has the HTTP
server. After years of `node_modules`, that feels like fresh air.

## The community half

I chair **Gopher Uzbekistan** under [FLOSS Uzbekistan](https://floss.uz):

- founded and maintain [gopher.uz](https://gopher.uz) — a home for Go developers
  in Uzbekistan,
- moderate the [Telegram community](https://t.me/goferuz),
- organise events, workshops and content around the Go ecosystem.

Running a community is its own skill, and it teaches the language too: nothing
exposes a shaky mental model faster than a room full of people asking "but why?".

## What I'm comfortable with

- `net/http` services, routing, middleware, graceful shutdown.
- Goroutines, channels, `context` cancellation — and knowing when a mutex is the
  simpler answer.
- Errors as values: wrapping with `%w`, `errors.Is` / `errors.As`.
- `go test` with table-driven tests, `go vet`, profiling basics.

## Still learning

- Larger project layout without cargo-culting somebody's `internal/` tree.
- Database access patterns I'd actually defend (`sqlc`, `pgx`) over an ORM habit
  imported from the JS world.
- Generics in real code, not in blog examples.

## Sukūn: stop on the letter

A small circle above a letter — <Ar>ـْ</Ar> — means **no vowel**. You close
the syllable on it: <Syl l="ba" k="s" />, not *aba*.

A letter with sukūn always leans on the syllable before it. That's how
Arabic makes closed syllables like *bah-r* or *qal-b*:

<Words list="bahr shams qalb bint shukr" />

The grid pairs each letter with a vowel (left) and with a sukūn after *a*
(right) so you can hear the difference:

## Shadda: say it twice

A small **w**-shape above a letter — <Ar>ـّ</Ar> — **doubles** it. The first
copy closes the syllable before, the second opens the next one:
*ḥam-mām*, *mu-ʿal-lim*. Hold the consonant a moment longer.

The shadda still wears a vowel: fatḥa above it, kasra just under it (or
under the letter), ḍamma above.

<Words list="umm hammam muallim dukkan sukkar" />

Notice how many of these Uzbek kept: *hammom, muallim, do‘kon*. The doubled
consonant is why *muallim* has that long middle.

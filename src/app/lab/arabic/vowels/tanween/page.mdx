Double a vowel mark at the **end** of a word and it gains an **n** sound you
never see written:

- two fatḥas <Syl l="ba" k="an" /> — *-an* (usually with a silent alif after it)
- two kasras <Syl l="ba" k="in" /> — *-in*
- two ḍammas <Syl l="ba" k="un" /> — *-un*

Tanwīn roughly means "a/an": <Ar>كِتَاب</Ar> is "book", <Ar>كِتَابٌ</Ar> is
"a book".

## Words you hear every day

Some of the most common Arabic words end in tanwīn — and one of them is a
word Uzbek uses all the time:

<Words list="shukran ahlan afwan jiddan mathalan daiman kitabun qalamin" />

*Masalan* and *doimo* are *mathalan* and *dāʾiman* with the tanwīn worn
smooth. The alif after the two fatḥas is written but not read: it's a seat,
not a vowel.

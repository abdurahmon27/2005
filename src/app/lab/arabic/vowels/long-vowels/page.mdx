Three letters do double duty. Most of the time they're consonants, but after
the matching short vowel they become **long vowels** — the same sound held
for **two beats**:

| Short | + letter | Long |
|---|---|---|
| fatḥa <Syl l="ba" k="a" /> | + alif <Ar>ا</Ar> | <Syl l="ba" k="aa" /> |
| kasra <Syl l="ba" k="i" /> | + yāʾ <Ar>ي</Ar> | <Syl l="ba" k="ii" /> |
| ḍamma <Syl l="ba" k="u" /> | + wāw <Ar>و</Ar> | <Syl l="ba" k="uu" /> |

The length is meaning, not style. Say a short vowel where a long one belongs
and you've said another word.

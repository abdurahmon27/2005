Arabic is joined writing, so most letters have **four shapes**: alone, at the
start of a word, in the middle, at the end. It's less than it sounds — the
start and middle shapes are usually the alone shape with its tail cut off.

## The six that never join forward

<Ar>ا د ذ ر ز و</Ar> — **alif, dāl, dhāl, rā, zāy, wāw** join to the letter
*before* them but never to the letter *after*. Whatever comes next starts
fresh, in its start shape. That's why <Ar>نور</Ar> has a gap after the
<Ar>و</Ar>, and why a word like <Ar>دار</Ar> doesn't join at all.

When you see a gap inside a word, look left of it: one of these six is there.

## The shapes that fool everyone

- <Ar>ه</Ar> changes the most: <Ar>هـ ـهـ ـه</Ar>. In the middle it looks like
  two small loops.
- <Ar>ع</Ar> and <Ar>غ</Ar> close up in the middle: <Ar>ـعـ</Ar>.
- <Ar>ك</Ar> loses its little mark at the start and middle: <Ar>كـ ـكـ</Ar>.
- <Ar>ي</Ar> and <Ar>ن</Ar> at the start look like <Ar>ب</Ar> — count the dots.
- <Ar>لا</Ar> — lām followed by alif — gets its own joined shape. It reads *lā*.

Arabic phoneticians don't say "make a k sound"; they say *where* to make it. Each
letter has a **makhraj** — an exit point — and once you know the place, the
sound follows.

Press a number on the head, or a row in the list. Then press the letters and
copy them, feeling where your tongue is.

<Makharij />

## The pairs that matter most

An Uzbek or English ear merges these. Arabic doesn't — swap them and the
word changes meaning.

| Light | Heavy / deeper | Where the difference is |
|---|---|---|
| <Syl l="hha" /> soft h | <Syl l="ha" /> tight h | bottom of the throat vs. middle |
| <Syl l="hamza" /> catch | <Syl l="ayn" /> squeeze | a stop vs. a voiced squeeze |
| <Syl l="kaf" /> k | <Syl l="qaf" /> q | the further back, the deeper |
| <Syl l="sin" /> s | <Syl l="sad" /> ṣ | tongue back down vs. raised |
| <Syl l="ta" /> t | <Syl l="tta" /> ṭ | same |
| <Syl l="dhal" /> dh | <Syl l="dha" /> ẓ | same |
| <Syl l="dal" /> d | <Syl l="dad" /> ḍ | tip vs. side of the tongue |

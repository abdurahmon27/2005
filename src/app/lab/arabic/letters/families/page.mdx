Early Arabic was written with almost **no dots**.
Readers knew the language, so the bare skeleton was enough. Dots came
later to tell look-alikes apart — and that history is the best shortcut for
learning the alphabet today.

Each card below starts with the **skeleton** (dashed box) and shows the
letters made from it. Press a letter to hear its name.

<Families />

## The dot rules

- **Count the dots, then look where they are.** ب has one below, ت two
  above, ث three above — same tooth.
- **Under, inside, over.** ج has its dot inside the cup, ح none, خ on top.
- **One dot or two** is the whole difference between ف and ق.

That leaves seven loners — ا ك ل م ن ه و — plus ي, which looks like a ب at
the start of a word but has its own tail at the end.

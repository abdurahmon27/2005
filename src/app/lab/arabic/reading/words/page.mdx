Every Arabic syllable starts with a consonant and has one vowel. That gives a
reliable method for any new word:

1. **Start at the right.** Find the first letter and its mark.
2. **Cut after every vowel** — or after a sukūn, which closes the syllable.
3. **Read each piece**, then **say them together** without stopping.

Take <Ar>مَكْتَب</Ar>: *mak* (mīm + fatḥa, kāf + sukūn) · *tab* (tāʾ + fatḥa,
bāʾ). *Maktab*. Uzbek kept it whole.

Or <Ar>مُعَلِّم</Ar>: *mu* · *ʿal* (the shadda closes this one…) · *lim*
(…and opens this one). *Muʿallim*.

## Practice — read, then press

Cover the Latin with your hand if you have to. Read the word out loud, then
press it to check.

## The last vowel is often skipped

When a word ends a sentence, the final short vowel is usually dropped:
*kataba* becomes *katab*, *kitābun* becomes *kitāb*. That's why the
dictionary forms in this course end on a consonant.

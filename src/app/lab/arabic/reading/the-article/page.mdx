<Ar>ٱلْ</Ar> — alif and lām — is the word **"the"**, glued to the front of a
noun. How you say it depends on the letter after it.

## Moon letters: say the l

Before these fourteen letters the l is pronounced, and it carries a sukūn:

<p><Ar>ا ب ج ح خ ع غ ف ق ك م ه و ي</Ar></p>

<Words list="al-qamar al-kitab al-bab al-maktab" />

They're called *moon* letters because <Ar>ٱلْقَمَر</Ar>, "the moon", is the
model.

## Sun letters: the l disappears

Before the other fourteen, the l is **written but not said**. Instead the
next letter doubles — you'll see a shadda on it and no sukūn on the lām:

<p><Ar>ت ث د ذ ر ز س ش ص ض ط ظ ل ن</Ar></p>

<Words list="ash-shams an-nur ad-dars as-salam" />

Named after <Ar>ٱلشَّمْس</Ar>, "the sun": *ash-shams*, never *al-shams*.

A quick test: every sun letter is made with the **tip of the tongue**, the
same place as the l. The l simply melts into it.

## The disappearing a

The alif of *al-* only sounds at the start of a sentence. After another word
it goes silent and the word before slides straight into the l. You'll often
see it written with a small mark on top, <Ar>ٱ</Ar>, which means "skip me
when joined":

<Words list="bil-qalam fil-bayt" />

*bi* + *al-qalam* → *bil-qalam*. *fī* + *al-bayt* → *fil-bayt*.

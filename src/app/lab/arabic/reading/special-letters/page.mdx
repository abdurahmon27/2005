A handful of spellings break the "one letter, one sound" rule. They're
common, so it's worth meeting them one at a time.

## Tāʾ marbūṭa — <Ar>ة</Ar>

A hāʾ with two dots, only ever at the **end** of a word. It reads **-a** when
you stop on it, and **-at** when the word runs on. It usually marks a feminine
noun — and it's the reason so many Uzbek words end in *-at*: *rahmat,
tabiat, hayot*.

<Words list="madrasa jamia rahma tabia" />

## Alif maqṣūra — <Ar>ى</Ar>

A yāʾ **without dots** at the end of a word. It reads as a long **ā**, just
like an alif.

<Words list="ala ila mana" />

## Hamza and its seats — <Ar>ء أ ؤ ئ</Ar>

Hamza (the little catch in the throat) rarely sits alone. It rides on an
alif, a wāw or a dotless yāʾ, depending on the vowels around it. The seat is
silent; only the hamza is heard.

<Words list="sual ras bir ma" />

## Lām-alif — <Ar>لا</Ar>

When lām is followed by alif, the two fuse into one shape. It always reads
**lā** — and it's also the word "no".

<Words list="la salam" />

[Botxona](/lab/ideas/botxona) has been live for one month, so here are the numbers
for that month. They're small, and they're real. Everything below comes straight
from the production database, and my own account and test payments are left out.

## The numbers

| | 1 November |
|---|---|
| **Users** | 96 |
| **Bots uploaded** | 71 |
| **Bots running** | 43 |
| **Paying customers** | 3 |
| **Paid subscriptions** | 4, all on Start |
| **MRR** | **156 000 so'm** (about $13) |
| **Spent on ads** | 0 |

The MRR is four Start subscriptions at 39 000 so'm each. One customer pays for
two bots. It's barely a coffee a day. But it's money that strangers sent by card
transfer to a platform with no launch post, after watching their own bot run for
15 minutes.

## Growth, week by week

Signups per week, by the Monday each week started:

| Week of | New users |
|---|---|
| 28 Sep | 3 |
| 5 Oct | 3 |
| 12 Oct | 22 |
| 19 Oct | 25 |
| 26 Oct | 45 |

The curve bent in the middle of October, and nothing I did caused it. I didn't
run an ad, post in a channel or ask a friend to share. The only thing that
changed is that AI assistants started recommending Botxona.

## Most users come from AI

[The first user](/lab/ideas/botxona/first-user) found Botxona by asking ChatGPT
where to deploy a bot. It turns out she wasn't an accident: **most of the people
who signed up this month came the same way.** Someone asks ChatGPT (or Claude,
or Gemini) "where can I host my Telegram bot in Uzbekistan?" and gets a link.

That changes what marketing means for me right now:

- **The docs are the ad.** The pages AIs read (the docs, the
  [prompt library](https://botxona.uz/prompts), the pricing) have to be
  current and true, because an AI repeats whatever it read last time. I found
  ChatGPT quoting prices that were weeks out of date, so the site now tells
  crawlers when a page changed instead of waiting to be re-read.
- **It's unmeasured.** There's an AI-launch flow where an assistant hands over
  the code and the user just pastes a token. It has been used twice. People
  don't use it: the AI recommends the site, and they sign up like anyone else.
  So "from AI" is something users tell me, not a column in the database. Next on
  the list: ask on signup.
- **No ads yet, on purpose.** Paying for traffic before the product holds up
  would mean paying to show more people the bugs. The free channel is working;
  the money can wait until the evenings stop going into fixes.

## A second server

The box Botxona started on is shared with
[Slaydxona](/lab/ideas/slaydxona), and by late October it was getting tight. So I bought a
second one: **6 vCPU, 12 GB RAM, 200 GB disk**. On 31 October it became the main
server. The control panel, the database and a new runner node live there now.

The move was done without stopping anyone's bot. The old server didn't go away.
It stays as the second node, its bots are drained over to the new one, and the
control plane reaches it through a relay. Two nodes is also the first step
towards the platform not depending on any single machine, which matters once
people are paying for their bots to stay up.

## What the month taught me

- **AI is the distribution channel.** I thought that after the first user; a
  month of signups says the same thing.
- **3 of 96 pay.** Free trials and the free catalogue tier do their job. Getting
  from "my bot runs" to "I pay for it" is the step to work on.
- **The real bots are the test suite.** 71 bots written by other people found
  more edge cases than my own test bots ever did, and most of my evenings went
  into those.

**Status: shipped, and it was never a side project.** FilterCam is the camera
filter module we added to the **streaming section of [dona.uz](https://dona.uz)**
— the live-shopping marketplace where sellers go on air and show a product in
real time while buyers ask questions and order inside the app.

## Why it exists

On a live-shopping marketplace the scarce resource isn't buyers, it's **sellers
who actually go live**. A stream that doesn't happen is inventory that doesn't
move. And the most common reason a seller doesn't press "go live" has nothing to
do with the product — it's that they don't want to spend an hour looking at
themselves on camera.

So the filter isn't decoration on top of the camera. It's supply-side retention:
it lowers the cost of being on air. That's the whole business case, and it's the
reason this got built properly instead of being bolted on.

One thing I want to be precise about, because I framed it wrong to myself at
first: **this was not a JavaScript feature.** Nothing interesting about it lives
in the JS layer. JS opens the camera and moves sliders; everything that matters
happens per frame on the GPU, in native code.

## What it does

- **Beauty** — GPU skin smoothing applied *only* to the face: MediaPipe Face Mesh
  (478 landmarks) builds a mask from the face oval with the eyes, brows and lips
  punched out, then an OpenGL blur/composite pass smooths just that region.
  Alongside it: glow, clarity (even tone / redness) and warmth.
- **Reshape** — eye-enlarge, nose-slim and face-slim liquify warps, each scaled to
  the tracked face.
- **Sprite overlays** — the same landmark pipeline anchors an image to the face and
  follows position, scale and head roll in real time (the demo app wears a
  mustache).
- Captures save the **filtered** frame — what the seller sees is what goes out.

Android runs **Kotlin + OpenGL ES**, iOS runs **Swift + Metal + Vision**, both
behind the same JS component, tracking up to five faces at once.

## What it cost me to learn

- **The frame budget is the spec.** At 30fps you have ~33ms for capture, landmark
  detection, mask, blur, warp and composite. Every design decision is downstream
  of that number.
- **Two platforms, two shader languages, one API.** Parity between Metal and
  OpenGL is harder than either pipeline on its own — the same slider has to mean
  the same thing on both, and it still drifts.
- **Native modules changed how I read React Native.** See
  [React Native](/lab/skills/react-native) for the concept map.
- I built the iOS half **without owning a Mac**, from NixOS — see
  [NixOS & Linux](/lab/skills/nixos) and
  [the write-up](/blog/ios-on-nixos).

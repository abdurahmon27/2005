**Status: paused at Phase 0.** Last commit in August 2026; the foundation is
scaffolded, the content schema is designed, nothing is live.

Duolingo × Brilliant, open source, localized in Uzbek, Russian and English —
with the flagship course being **Russian for Uzbek speakers**, which is a thing
millions of people need and nobody has built well.

## The one idea worth keeping

Most language apps are built by people whose interface language, first language
and target language are all obvious. Ours never are. So Oris keeps three axes
apart everywhere, and refuses to let them collapse into one:

| Axis | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **UI locale** | the language of the interface | `uz` |
| **Source (L1)** | the language a course teaches *from* | `uz` |
| **Target (L2)** | the language being taught | `ru` |

Course identity is `(subject, L1, L2)`. `ru-from-uz` and `ru-from-en` are
separate trees sharing a content skeleton; a non-language course simply has
`L2 = null`. It sounds like bookkeeping until you try to build it the other way
and discover that "Russian" means four different products depending on who is
asking.

The second decision I'd keep: **content is data, not code.** Every exercise is a
JSON document validated against a versioned schema, reviewable in git — which
is what makes "anyone can add a locale or author a course" a real contribution
path rather than a sentence in a README.

## Why it stopped

Not because it was wrong. Because [maymun](/lab/ideas/maymun) is the same
audience answered a different way — generate the lesson instead of authoring it
— and I could not honestly work on both. One of them had to go to the back of
the queue, and the one with 200 hand-authored exercises ahead of it lost.

The uncomfortable version, which [the roast](/lab/me/roast) puts better than I
would: I don't finish things, I promote them to interesting ideas. This note is
me writing down *which* idea it was promoted past, so the decision is at least
visible.

## What would bring it back

- maymun's Phase 0 voice gate failing — in which case authored content beats
  generated narration and Oris is the right product after all.
- Someone else wanting to own the content side. The whole point of the schema
  is that authoring does not have to be me.
